Team 4  |  Wallonia + Liège–Maastricht–Aachen triangle  |  All links opened on 29 Oct 2026  |  External sources, NOT challenge data (Annex 2 "EFAS" = hazard maps, a different product)
Wording for the brief: GEV estimates how rare a flood is (its return period) under an assumed stable climate. It does not predict floods. Say "how rare" or "return period", never "prediction".

2. Published numbers to compare against

  • ENW, "Hoogwater 2021 – Feiten en Duiding", §2.4 pp. 21–22 – enwinfo.nl (PDF)
    Borgharen, annual maxima (GEV, Gumbel, GRADE): ~1:100 to 1:200 yr. Summer half-year GEV: ~1:600 (CI ~1:100 to 1:10,000). Sint Pieter peak 3,260 m³/s (3,200–3,400), p. 30. Geul, Geleenbeek, Roer: 1:100 to 1:1,000.
  • Rijkswaterstaat, "Analyse hoogwater Maas juli 2021" – open.rijkswaterstaat.nl
    Borgharen Dorp peak ~3,284 m³/s, about 1:125 yr (official working curve).
  • Journée et al. 2023, HESS 27:3169 (RMI) – hess.copernicus.org/articles/27/3169/2023
    1–3-day totals well above the 200-yr level, up to +90% for 2–3-day totals in the Vesdre basin. Up to 3 h: ≤ 1:30 yr. So: long soaking rain, not a cloudburst. RMI 200-yr levels for Jalhay: 108.8 mm (1 d), 138.6 mm (2 d), 150.6 mm (3 d).
  • Tradowsky et al. 2023, Climatic Change 176:90 (peer-reviewed World Weather Attribution study) – doi.org/10.1007/s10584-023-03502-7
    Regionally ≥ 1 in 400 yr; climate change made the rain 1.2–9× more likely and 3–19% heavier. No local Meuse return periods.
  • Rongen et al. 2024, HESS 28:2831 – hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/2831/2024
    Borgharen: 120 yr (annual max) and 600 yr (summer). These "were derived including the July 2021 event itself"; before the event it would have looked rarer.
  • Ludwig et al. 2023, NHESS 23:1287 (Ahr, Germany: method example only) – nhess.copernicus.org/articles/23/1287/2023
    Adding historical floods back to 1804 changes the return period a lot (Altenahr ~HQ10,000).
  • Wallonia, "Annexe F – Ajustement GEV" (Oct 2021) – wallonie.be (PDF)
    14 pages of GEV plots per gauge (hourly discharge, 95% CI, 2021 peak). Plots only, no tables; parent report not named.

Not verified: Dewals et al. 2021 (Water), not opened; Chaudfontaine Q100 ≈ 255 m³/s (symposium slide only); Deltares/RWS "Evaluation of discharge extremes in the Meuse" (Dec 2023), results not read.

3. Traps to state in the brief

  1. Chaudfontaine 2021 is an estimate. SPW stores 676.5 m³/s; the gauge went off its scale, and ENW gives ~520 m³/s (model estimate).
  2. Daily means hide the peak. Borgharen: 3,017 (daily mean) vs 3,284 m³/s (peak). Prefer hourly/instant maxima.
  3. Record length. With 50–60 years, anything beyond ~1:200 is very uncertain (RMI stops at 200 yr). Always show a confidence range.
  4. Fit with and without 2021 and show both.
  5. Block choice matters (calendar year, hydrological year from 1 Nov, summer half-year). Say which one you used.
  6. scipy sign: scipy.stats.genextreme uses c = −ξ. A heavy tail (ξ > 0) appears as c < 0.

4. Minimal plan (one day)

  1. Gauges: Vesdre at Chaudfontaine (strongest story), Ourthe at Tabreux (2021: 542 m³/s vs 433 in 1993), Meuse at Borgharen (cross-border, has published numbers). Optional: Inde at Eschweiler (329.5 m³/s in July 2021).
  2. Fetch: one KiWIS request per Walloon gauge (annual or hydrological-year max); ddlpy for Borgharen.
  3. Fit: genextreme.fit without 2021, then with 2021; return period of the 2021 peak T = 1 / (1 − F(x)); 1,000 bootstrap resamples for the CI.
  4. Check: compare with ENW and Rijkswaterstaat (Borgharen) and Annexe F (Wallonia). Log the comparison in the AI sheet.
