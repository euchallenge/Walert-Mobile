# walert, Android side: 1-minute talk for policy makers (Team 4)

About 150 words, about 1 minute at a calm pace. [Brackets] = what to tap on the phone (scrcpy on the projector).
Teammates cover the map, the analysis and the recommendation; this minute is the phone in a resident's hand.

---

This is walert on a resident's phone.

[Open the app → Français] The first time it opens, you pick your language: French, Dutch, German or English.
Then an 18-second video says what to do in a flood, based on the Crisis Centre's advice. No reading needed.
The app remembers the language, so in a crisis it opens straight in it.

[I live here → ♿ Elderly or disabled → Continue] One switch for those who need it: big text, read aloud,
and shelters without stairs. Then three big buttons, nothing else.

[Closest shelter] One: the closest candidate shelter, by a walking route that stays dry even in a
1-in-500-year flood model. With the switch on, the route avoids stairs. We always say: not official,
check with your municipality.

[Water, power, gas] Two: a mayor told us residents had to search three websites. Now it's one tap: the right
number for their municipality, and tapping it calls it.

[Messages] Three: your messages. Signed by your municipality, sent with a verification code, so residents
know it's really you.

And their location never leaves the phone.

---

## For questions
- Shelters: candidates from our own analysis (teammate's routes, 1-in-500-year scenario), not official reception
  centres. Never say "safe" or "predict".
- Utility numbers: official operator pages (CILE, SWDE, RESA), demo municipalities only (Visé, Liège, Esneux,
  Pepinster); elsewhere the app says "check your bill or your municipality".
- Messages: a demo server on our laptop today; for real, it would run on the municipality's side.
- If the server is offline: show the messages screen and say the line above, no live send.
- Data covers 66 municipalities in Wallonia only (EMSR518).
